Training

Alawen does not run generic training. Each programme starts from the need that triggers the course, then is adjusted to the actual level of your participants. The length depends on your context and expectations.

Enterprise blockchain

Operations, development and security, on the protocol you use. The two programmes below are examples already delivered, over three days.

Hyperledger Besu: operations and development

  • Day 1: Besu architecture, QBFT and IBFT 2.0 consensus, starting a private network with Docker, monitoring with Prometheus and Grafana, troubleshooting.
  • Day 2: smart contracts with Foundry, from writing to testing and deployment, EVM basics, an introduction to Yul, common security mistakes.
  • Day 3: application integration in Node.js, Java or .NET, sending transactions, listening to events, resilience in production.

Blockchain security on Hyperledger Besu

  • Day 1: where blockchain security comes from, what QBFT guarantees, finality, what cryptographic proofs and interoperability change.
  • Day 2: Solidity and EVM flaws, randomness, bridges and interoperability, real hacks dissected and replayed in a controlled environment.
  • Day 3: a CTF-style exercise, reconnaissance, static analysis, execution traces, writing exploits against vulnerable contracts.

Agentic AI

A course that gets your teams practising with AI agents fast, and helps them catch up on deployment if they have fallen behind.

Practice first

Most of the time is spent in workshops, on cases drawn from your business. Theory comes in support, when a workshop calls for it.

What you need to deploy

The programme starts from what your team already does with AI and from what blocks the move to production. Participants leave knowing how to pick a use case, build the agent and put it into service.

A level that holds

Tools and models change fast. After the session, a delegated tech watch tells participants what has changed and what it means for their work.

A peer group

The participants of a session form a group that carries on after the course, to share their cases, their questions and what worked. Everyone grows with the others.

How a course runs

  • A preparation meeting and a form to learn each participant's profile, level and goals.
  • About 70% hands-on workshops and 30% theory.
  • Ten participants at most per session. Beyond that, a second session is organised.
  • Digital course material and corrected exercises handed out at the end.
  • At your premises, in French or English.
